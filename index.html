Threat Library

Know the shape of a scam.

The tactics repeat. Once you recognize the pattern, it loses its power.

Account Suspension Scams

A message claims your bank, email, or social account will be locked unless you act immediately.

Package Delivery Phishing

A text claims a parcel couldn't be delivered and asks you to pay a small fee through a suspicious link.

Spoofed Caller ID

Scammers disguise their real number to appear as a bank, government agency, or saved contact.

Romance & Trust Scams

A new online connection builds trust quickly and then asks for money or an investment.

Lookalike Domains

URLs swap letters or add extra words to imitate trusted brands, such as using 0 instead of O.

Fake Job & Payment Offers

An unsolicited job promises high pay and then asks for personal information or an upfront payment.

One-Ring Callback Scams

A single ring from an unfamiliar international area code baits you into calling back a premium-rate number.

Tech Support Impersonation

An unsolicited call or pop-up claims your device is infected and asks for remote access or payment.

How It Works

From paste to verdict.

Four steps take you from an uneasy feeling to a clear, evidence-based read on whatever landed in your inbox.

1
Paste what you received
Drop in a message, link, phone number, email sender, or profile — exactly as you received it.
2
ScamLens scans for patterns
Each tab runs its own checks — URL structure for links, area codes for phone numbers, sender domains for email, and wording patterns for messages and profiles.
3
Structured assessment
ScamLens returns a risk score, a category, the specific warning signs found, and recommended next steps.
4
You make an informed decision
You see exactly which parts of the message appear suspicious and why, helping you decide whether to trust it.
What We Look For
⚠️
Urgency & Pressure
Legitimate companies rarely force you to act immediately via unsolicited messages.
🛡️
Impersonation
Scammers pretend to be banks, government agencies, or trusted companies.
🔑
Credential Requests
No legitimate service asks for passwords, OTPs, or PINs via message.
💳
Payment Scams
Advance fees, unusual payment methods, or rewards that seem too good to be true.
🌐
Domain Structure
IP addresses, punycode, excess hyphens, and character swaps used to imitate real brands.
📟
Contact Channel
Certain delivery channels — like unfamiliar area codes or spam folders — carry more baseline risk.
About ScamLens

A closer look, before you trust.

ScamLens is a triage tool that helps you sanity-check messages, websites, phone numbers, emails, and profiles that feel slightly off.

Each category runs its own detection logic: URL and domain structure for links and email senders, area-code and impersonation patterns for phone numbers, and wording patterns for messages and profiles.

Your scan history is saved locally to this tool so you can revisit past checks — it isn't shared anywhere else.

Treat every result as a second opinion and use your own judgment for anything involving money or personal information.
